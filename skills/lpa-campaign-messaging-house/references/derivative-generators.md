# Derivative Generators

Structures for `SECTION 4` of the Campaign Messaging House. Each converts the spine (Section 2) and the claim ledger (Section 3) into an asset shape.

**Binding rule for all three generators:** derivatives carry no new claims. Every line cites the claim IDs it draws on, in the form `[CLM-P2.1]`. A line that cites nothing is either unsupported or unnecessary.

Apply the language constraints from `SKILL.md` to every drafted line. They are not optional here — this is where the copy gets written.

---

## 1. PAISA — direct response

Source: Roy Furr. Use for landing pages, cold email, paid social, and any asset whose job is a single conversion.

| Beat | Content | Draws from |
|---|---|---|
| **P — Problem** | The acute, burning problem, in the buyer's words | Step 1 Insight; `Customer Interview` verbatim |
| **A — Agitate** | The deeper cost of leaving it unsolved: what compounds, what gets missed, what it costs internally | Step 2 cons; step 1 |
| **I — Invalidate** | Why the common alternatives fail — mechanism, not dismissal | Step 2 alternatives; `Attack Matrix` |
| **S — Solve** | The product as the resolution, tied to the pillars | Steps 4-6; pillar claims |
| **A — Ask** | One specific productized offer and CTA | Step 7 ask |

**Rules.**
- One sequence per campaign, not one per channel. Channels get length cuts of the same sequence.
- Agitate names consequences the buyer has felt. Invented stakes read as manipulation and kill trust.
- Invalidate attacks the *approach*, using a generic category label externally — never a named competitor.
- Solve cites at least one `Proven` claim. A PAISA built only on `Provisional` claims does not ship.
- The Ask matches step 7 exactly. Do not soften or multiply it here.

**Length target.** Problem 1-2 sentences, Agitate 2-3, Invalidate 2-3, Solve 3-4, Ask 1-2.

## 2. Contrast Loop — video, keynote, visual

Source: Nancy Duarte. Use for campaign video, event keynotes, launch webinars, and long-form visual narrative.

The structure alternates between two states, building tension through repetition, then resolves.

**Beat sheet — fixed order:**

1. `WHAT IS` — current reality; empathize, establish the baseline. Draws on step 1 and step 2 status quo.
2. `WHAT COULD BE` — first glimpse of the better state. Draws on step 3 criterion 1.
3. `WHAT IS` — why the gap persists; the obstacle. Draws on step 2 cons.
4. `WHAT COULD BE` — a sharper vision, raised stakes. Draws on step 3 criteria 2-3.
5. `WHAT IS` — the final and most acute cost of staying put.
6. `WHAT COULD BE` — the full picture, now specific.
7. `NEW BLISS` — the product as the bridge; resolve the tension. Draws on steps 4-6 and the pillar claims.

**Rules.**
- Minimum three complete oscillations before the resolution. Two makes the tension flat.
- The product does not appear until beat 7. Earlier product mentions collapse the structure into a demo.
- Each `WHAT COULD BE` must escalate on the one before it. Restating is not escalating.
- `NEW BLISS` describes the buyer's changed state, not the product's capabilities.
- Cite claim IDs on beat 7 lines.

**Length target.** 60-90 seconds for a social cut, 3-5 minutes for a campaign film, 12-18 minutes for a keynote. Beat count stays 7 at every length.

## 3. The 6 Hooks — headlines and subject lines

Sources: Dan Norris, Purna Virji. Use for subject lines, headlines, ad copy, and social openers.

Generate **two candidates per hook type — 12 total** — then narrow to 3 recommended.

| # | Hook | Definition | Constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Contrarian** | Argues the opposite of accepted convention | Must be defensible with a `Proven` claim. A contrarian hook you cannot back is a liability |
| 2 | **Surprising** | An unexpected fact or statistic that contradicts assumption | Cite the source in the body copy. No orphan statistics |
| 3 | **Overcome Objections** | Tackles the primary friction point head-on | Pull the objection from step 7, not from imagination |
| 4 | **Guarantee** | A credible promise of a specific outcome | Legal exposure lives here. Only from `Proven` claims |
| 5 | **Interest** | Specific, un-rounded metrics that build credibility | Un-rounded numbers only. **Never a time-saved or speed multiple** — use capability or outcome metrics |
| 6 | **How To with a Twist** | Benefit format qualified by a hard condition ("even if X") | The condition must be a real constraint the ICP has |

**Rules.**
- Every hook cites the claim ID it rests on. A hook citing an `Unsupported` claim is discarded, not softened.
- No adverbs. No hype vocabulary. No named competitors.
- Hook 5 is where the efficiency-multiplier habit resurfaces. Reject any candidate whose number is a time saving or a speed multiple, and replace it with a capability or outcome number.
- Narrow to 3 by pillar coverage, not by preference: the recommended set should cover at least two different pillars.
- Send all 3 to the `Bar Test` verbatim before use.

## Coverage check

Run after generating all three:

1. List every claim ID in the ledger. Mark which appear in at least one derivative.
2. Uncited claims: cut the claim, or cut the pillar if none of its claims survive.
3. Derivative lines citing no claim: remove them or trace them to a claim.
4. Confirm no `Unsupported` claim appears anywhere in Section 4.
