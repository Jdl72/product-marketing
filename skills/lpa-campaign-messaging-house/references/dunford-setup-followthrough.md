# Dunford Setup & Follow-Through — Step Definitions

Source framework: April Dunford, *Sales Pitch*. This is the narrative spine for `SECTION 2` of the Campaign Messaging House.

Do not reorder these steps. Do not merge steps 1-3 with steps 4-7. The split is the framework.

---

## THE SETUP — market context, no product

The Setup earns the right to talk about the product. Nothing in steps 1-3 mentions the product, the brand, or a feature.

### Step 1 — Insight

**Definition.** One core truth about the customer's situation that makes your differentiated value matter. It is the reverse of your differentiated value, stated as a market fact.

**How to derive it.** Take the strongest entry from step 5. Invert it into the condition that makes it necessary. If your differentiated value is "campaign decisions grounded in retail-specific search data," the insight is "the signals that decide retail media outcomes do not exist in general ad platforms."

**Rules.**
- Two sentences maximum.
- Must be true whether or not your product exists.
- A buyer who has never heard of you should nod at it.
- Must be arguable. If nobody in the market would dispute it, it is a platitude, not an insight.

**Fails when.** It reads as a product benefit with the brand name removed. It states a problem everyone already agrees on. It requires believing your product is good.

**Write this step last within the Setup.** Draft steps 2 and 3 first, then invert the value and check it against them.

---

### Step 2 — Alternatives

**Definition.** The real options the buyer has today, with honest pros and cons for each.

**Rules.**
- Include the status quo: manual work, spreadsheets, an existing internal process, hiring someone, doing nothing.
- Three to five alternatives. Fewer means you have not looked; more means you are padding.
- Each alternative gets at least one genuine pro. An alternative with no upside is a straw man and buyers will catch it.
- Name switching cost where it explains why a weak alternative persists.
- Name competitors by name here. This section is internal.

**Fails when.** Every alternative is described as bad. The status quo is missing. The cons are written in your product's vocabulary rather than the buyer's.

---

### Step 3 — Perfect World

**Definition.** What an ideal solution would look like, independent of any vendor. These are buying criteria, and getting agreement on them is the point of the entire Setup.

**Rules.**
- Three to five criteria. These become the campaign's pillars, so the count matters.
- Each criterion is stated as a capability the buyer wants, not a feature you ship.
- Each must be a criterion the buyer would state without prompting.
- Order them by how much the buyer cares, not by how well you score.
- It is acceptable — and credible — to include a criterion you do not lead on.

**Fails when.** Criteria are your feature list rewritten in buyer voice. A criterion only exists because you happen to satisfy it. There are more than five.

**Test.** Show the criteria to someone who has not seen your product and ask them to name vendors that meet them. If your product is the only conceivable answer, the criteria are rigged and the Setup will not persuade.

## THE FOLLOW-THROUGH — your solution

Only now does the product appear.

### Step 4 — Introduction

**Definition.** A formal introduction of the product placed inside a defined market category.

**Rules.**
- One sentence: product name, market category, what it does.
- The category must be one the buyer already recognizes, or one you define in a single clause.
- No differentiation yet. That is step 5.

**Fails when.** The category is invented and unexplained. The sentence needs a diagram.

---

### Step 5 — Differentiated Value

**Definition.** How the product delivers on the step 3 criteria in ways others cannot replicate.

**Rules.**
- One entry per `Perfect World` criterion. Map each explicitly: `Criterion 2 → ...`.
- State the unique attribute *and* the value it produces. An attribute alone is a spec; value alone is a benefit anyone could claim.
- Ask "so what?" until the answer is commercially meaningful to the buyer's job.
- If a criterion has no differentiated answer, say so. Do not invent one — the honest gap is what makes the rest credible.

**Fails when.** Entries do not map to criteria. Every entry claims uniqueness. The value is a metric improvement with no capability behind it.

---

### Step 6 — Proof

**Definition.** Evidence that you deliver the step 5 value: customer results, data, case studies, third-party validation.

**Rules.**
- One proof point per step 5 entry, minimum.
- Name the source. Unattributed numbers do not count as proof.
- Prefer customer-reported outcomes over internal benchmarks.
- Mark anything you cannot source. It becomes a `Provisional` or `Unsupported` claim in the ledger.

**Fails when.** Proof is a logo wall with no outcome attached. Numbers appear with no source or date. The proof supports a different claim than the one it is attached to.

---

### Step 7 — Objections & The Ask

**Definition.** The silent objections addressed before they are raised, then one specific low-friction call to action.

**Rules.**
- Name three objections minimum. Cover at least one of: implementation effort, switching or migration cost, pricing, internal buy-in.
- Answer each with evidence or a mechanism, not reassurance.
- One ask. Not a menu.
- The ask must match the campaign's funnel stage from the GACCS brief. A cold-audience campaign asking for a demo is a mismatch.

**Fails when.** Objections are the easy ones you enjoy answering. The ask is "learn more." There are three CTAs competing.

---

## Spine integrity check

Run before building pillars:

1. Does any step 1-3 content name the product? If yes, move it to 4-7.
2. Does every `Perfect World` criterion have a step 5 entry? Unanswered criteria mean the Setup promises what the product does not deliver.
3. Does every step 5 entry answer a criterion? Orphans mean you are selling something the buyer never agreed to want — add the criterion to step 3 or cut the entry.
4. Does every step 5 entry have step 6 proof? Unproven entries carry into the ledger as `Provisional`, never as headline claims.
5. Is the step 7 ask consistent with the GACCS goal metric?
