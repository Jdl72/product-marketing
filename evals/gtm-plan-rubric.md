# GTM Plan Rubric

## Purpose

Evaluate whether a GTM plan turns positioning and buyer evidence into an executable launch or growth plan.

## Scoring scale

- `1 = poor`
- `3 = acceptable`
- `5 = strong`

## Dimensions

### 1. Goal and audience clarity

Question:

Does the plan define what it is trying to achieve and for whom?

High score:

- launch goal is explicit and measurable
- target segments are named clearly
- the plan does not confuse audience with channel

Low score:

- goals are vague
- the audience is generic
- the plan assumes one motion works for everyone

### 2. Strategic coherence

Question:

Does the core message connect cleanly to the plan's channels, sequence, and programs?

High score:

- core message is consistent with the target audience
- audience sequence, channel strategy, and demand programs reinforce one another
- the plan reflects upstream positioning rather than improvising a new story

Low score:

- the message and programs feel disconnected
- the plan is a list of activities without strategy
- sequencing is arbitrary or absent

### 3. Dependency realism

Question:

Does the plan surface the operational dependencies required to execute it?

High score:

- enablement, content, owner, and timeline dependencies are explicit
- decision points and assumptions are visible
- the plan acknowledges what must happen before launch execution can work

Low score:

- dependencies are hidden
- ownership is unclear
- the plan assumes work will happen without assigning it

### 4. Measurement and risk quality

Question:

Does the plan define how success and failure will be recognized?

High score:

- measurement plan is concrete
- risks and assumptions are explicit
- the plan makes tradeoffs and uncertainty visible

Low score:

- the measurement plan is missing or generic
- risks are absent
- assumptions are buried or treated as facts

### 5. Execution readiness

Question:

Could a team use this plan without major restructuring?

High score:

- the plan is structured enough for execution
- owners and timeline are usable
- the GTM logic can be handed to marketing, sales, and cross-functional teams

Low score:

- the plan is conceptually interesting but operationally weak
- teams would need to rebuild the plan before acting
- key execution details are missing

## Pass / fail rule

Pass if:

- launch goal, target segments, core message, channel strategy, measurement plan, owners, and timeline are all present
- average score is at least `3`
- `measurement and risk quality` is at least `3`

Fail if:

- ownership or timeline is missing
- the plan has no measurement logic
- the plan is not connected to a clear audience and message
- major dependencies are absent

## Review notes to capture every time

- strongest strategic choice
- weakest operational area
- biggest launch risk
- downstream team most dependent on this plan
