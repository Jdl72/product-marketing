# Positioning Brief Rubric

## Purpose

Evaluate whether a positioning brief is clear, evidence-grounded, differentiated, and reusable across downstream GTM and sales work.

## Scoring scale

- `1 = poor`
- `3 = acceptable`
- `5 = strong`

## Dimensions

### 1. Segment and persona specificity

Question:

Is the target audience defined precisely enough to make the positioning usable?

High score:

- the brief names a clear target segment and target persona
- the segment and persona align with upstream PMM evidence
- the brief avoids collapsing materially different audiences into one story

Low score:

- the audience is generic or undefined
- the brief mixes multiple personas without distinction
- the target audience could apply to almost any buyer

### 2. Problem framing quality

Question:

Does the brief make the buyer's problem and status quo explicit?

High score:

- the core problem is concrete, evidence-backed, and commercially meaningful
- status quo alternatives are named and explained
- the problem framing reflects customer reality rather than internal product language

Low score:

- the problem is vague or abstract
- status quo alternatives are missing
- the problem reads like internal messaging instead of customer evidence

### 3. Differentiation clarity

Question:

Is the differentiated promise specific and defensible?

High score:

- the promise is distinct from the status quo and named alternatives
- unique attributes explain why the promise is credible
- the brief avoids undifferentiated category language

Low score:

- the promise could apply to competitors as easily as to this solution
- unique attributes are generic or missing
- the brief relies on broad claims like better, faster, or easier without explanation

### 4. Proof discipline

Question:

Are proof points tied to evidence rather than assertion?

High score:

- proof points are linked to an evidence basis or clearly framed as assumptions
- proof supports the differentiated promise directly
- unsupported claims are excluded

Low score:

- proof points are really slogans or features without support
- the brief overstates certainty
- major claims have no evidence basis

### 5. Downstream usability

Question:

Can the brief be reused in GTM, enablement, or campaign work without major reinterpretation?

High score:

- value proposition, objections, message pillars, and one-liner are internally consistent
- the brief is concise enough to reuse but complete enough to guide downstream teams
- the message structure is easy to adapt into other artifacts

Low score:

- the brief is internally contradictory
- key downstream fields are missing
- reuse would require re-deriving the core positioning logic

## Pass / fail rule

Pass if:

- target audience, core problem, differentiated promise, proof points, and evidence basis are all present
- the differentiated promise is supported by specific proof or explicitly marked assumptions
- average score is at least `3`
- `proof discipline` is at least `3`

Fail if:

- the target segment or target persona is missing
- the differentiated promise has no meaningful proof support
- the brief uses generic positioning language that is not tied to evidence
- the status quo alternatives are absent

## Review notes to capture every time

- strongest element of the positioning
- weakest element of the positioning
- biggest evidence gap
- downstream artifact most likely to benefit from this brief
