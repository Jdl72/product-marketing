# Battle Card Rubric

## Purpose

Evaluate whether a battle card helps a field team compete credibly without oversimplifying the market.

## Scoring scale

- `1 = poor`
- `3 = acceptable`
- `5 = strong`

## Dimensions

### 1. Competitive clarity

Question:

Does the card define the competitor and category context clearly?

High score:

- competitor, approach, and category are explicit
- the card makes the comparison frame clear
- the card avoids category confusion

Low score:

- the competitor context is unclear
- category framing is missing
- the card assumes the reader already knows the market structure

### 2. Trade-off honesty

Question:

Does the card represent both strengths and weaknesses without collapsing into propaganda?

High score:

- strengths and weaknesses are both present
- trade-offs are real and usable in the field
- when we win and when we lose are both explicit

Low score:

- weaknesses are hidden
- trade-offs are replaced by one-sided selling points
- the card pretends every deal is a good fit

### 3. Purchasing relevance

Question:

Does the card connect competition to the criteria buyers actually care about?

High score:

- key purchasing criteria are explicit
- our advantage is framed relative to those criteria
- the card helps sellers understand where the comparison matters most

Low score:

- purchasing criteria are vague or absent
- the card lists features without buyer relevance
- our advantage is asserted without comparison logic

### 4. Objection handling quality

Question:

Does the card help a seller respond responsibly to likely objections?

High score:

- objection handling is specific
- responses are grounded in proof
- the card does not encourage unsupported or reckless claims

Low score:

- objection handling is generic
- proof points do not support the responses
- the card encourages overclaiming

### 5. Source and confidence discipline

Question:

Can the card's claims be trusted and reviewed?

High score:

- sources are explicit
- proof points and confidence are present
- uncertain claims are visibly less certain

Low score:

- sources are missing
- confidence is absent
- the card treats weak signal as settled fact

## Pass / fail rule

Pass if:

- competitor, category, trade-offs, key purchasing criteria, our advantage, objection handling, sources, and confidence are all present
- average score is at least `3`
- `source and confidence discipline` is at least `3`

Fail if:

- the card has no sources
- the card hides weaknesses or when-we-lose scenarios
- proof points do not support the main competitive claims
- objection handling requires unsupported claims

## Review notes to capture every time

- strongest competitive insight
- weakest or riskiest claim
- biggest missing source
- field scenario where this card is most useful
